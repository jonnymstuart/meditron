Which four questions should I answer before buying an ultrasound system?

Answer these four questions and the shortlist usually narrows to two models.

1. Which exams do I mainly perform? A system for prenatal diagnostics needs different imaging modes (3D/4D, specialised measurement packages) than one for abdomen, thyroid and musculoskeletal work. Samsung splits its range accordingly: HERA for women's health, the R and V series for radiology and general imaging.

2. How high is my exam volume? At 20 or more exams a day, automated measurements and presets pay off because they save clicks and post-processing. At a few exams a week, a compact system is the more economical choice.

3. Which probes do I need? Convex for abdomen and obstetrics, linear for thyroid, vessels and musculoskeletal, endocavity for gynaecology and urology, sector (phased array) for cardiac. Probes account for a large share of the price and should be decided first.

4. How much space and budget do I have? A premium system with a 27-inch OLED monitor needs more floor space than a compact system on 56 × 63 cm. Financing and leasing change the calculation; more on that below.

What matters when choosing ultrasound probes?

The probe determines what a system delivers day to day. Three points are decisive:

  • Frequency range. Higher frequencies give more resolution at less penetration depth (thyroid, tendons, vessels); lower frequencies give more depth at less resolution (abdomen, obstetrics).
  • Form factor. Convex, linear, endocavity, sector. A typical general imaging practice starts with convex and linear; gynaecology adds endocavity; cardiology needs sector.
  • Number of active ports. If you switch probes often during an exam, several probes connected at once saves time.

Meditron configures the probe selection together with you on the basis of your exams, not from a catalogue.

What are the Swiss requirements before buying an ultrasound system?

Three things to check that are specific to Switzerland:

  • SGUM certificate of competence. To bill ultrasound examinations to health insurers, physicians need the certificate of competence (Fähigkeitsausweis) of the Swiss Society of Ultrasound in Medicine (SGUM) for the relevant module, for example abdomen, gynaecology and obstetrics, or musculoskeletal. Check the current requirements with SGUM before you invest.
  • Swissmedic and CE marking. Ultrasound systems are medical devices. Systems and probes sold by Meditron are CE-marked and placed on the Swiss market in line with Swissmedic requirements. Grey imports may not be.
  • DICOM and practice software. The system should send images and reports to your practice software or PACS. Meditron sets up the DICOM connection at installation.

Frequently asked questions

Which Samsung ultrasound system is best for a gynaecology practice?

The Samsung HERA Z20. It was developed for gynaecology and obstetrics and offers 2D, 3D/4D and colour imaging with AI-assisted workflows. Practices that mix gynaecology with other exams often choose the V8.

What is the difference between the Samsung V8 and V7?

The V8 relies on Crystal Architecture™ and Intelligent Assist for image quality and speed. The V7 adds quantification tools for abdomen and liver. Both share the same platform and probe selection.

How much space does a Samsung ultrasound system need?

Compact models such as the V4 and V5 stand on around 56 × 63 cm, premium systems such as the HERA Z20 and R20 on around 63 × 94 cm, each excluding room to move around the system.
