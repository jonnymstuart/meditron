Why is there no list price for a Samsung ultrasound system?

Because a medical ultrasound system is not a single product. The console is the base; the probes, the imaging software and the service agreement are chosen to match the exams a practice performs. Two practices buying the same Samsung V8 can end up with configurations that differ by a factor of two, because one needs a single convex probe for abdominal routine and the other needs four probes plus elastography and contrast imaging. That is why Samsung, like every ultrasound manufacturer, does not publish list prices, and why prices you find on foreign marketplaces rarely describe a comparable configuration.

What drives the price of an ultrasound system?

Five factors, in order of impact:

1. The model tier. Samsung's range runs from compact systems for routine diagnostics (V4, V5, V6) through mid-range general imaging systems (V7, V8) to premium platforms for women's health (HERA Z20) and radiology with intervention (R20). Monitor size, image processing and automation increase with each tier.

2. The probes. Probes are the largest variable after the console. A basic configuration has one or two probes (convex and linear); a gynaecology practice adds an endocavity probe; cardiology needs a sector probe; obstetrics may add a 3D/4D volume probe. Each probe is a separate line on the quote.

3. Software packages. Advanced features such as elastography (ElastoScan), contrast-enhanced ultrasound, liver quantification (EzHRI™, TAI™, TSI™) or automated obstetric measurements are licensed options. You pay only for the packages your exams need.

4. Service contract. A service agreement covers preventive maintenance, repairs, loan equipment and software updates over a set number of years. It can be included in the purchase or leasing price or contracted separately.

5. Financing. Purchase, leasing or a certified pre-owned system change the entry cost and the total cost; see the comparison below.

Delivery, installation, DICOM integration with your practice software and applications training by Meditron specialists are itemised in the quote, not a surprise afterwards.

What does an ultrasound system cost over its lifetime?

Practices usually keep an ultrasound system for seven to ten years, so the purchase price is only part of the cost. Four items decide the rest:

  • Service and maintenance. Probes are the component most likely to need repair or replacement. A service contract with probe cover protects against the largest unplanned expense.
  • Software updates. Samsung releases feature and security updates; whether they are included depends on the service agreement.
  • Training. A system the team uses to its full extent pays off faster. Meditron provides applications training at installation and on request afterwards.
  • Resale value. Samsung systems with a documented service history hold their value on the pre-owned market and can be traded in.

Four steps, usually within two weeks:

  2. We propose a configuration. Model, probes and packages matched to those exams, with alternatives at different price points.

Which questions should I ask any ultrasound supplier?

  • Is the quote itemised by console, probes, software packages, service, installation and training?
  • Which probes are included, and what does an additional probe cost?
  • What does the service contract cover, for how long, and does it include probes and loan equipment?
  • Are the system and probes CE-marked and placed on the Swiss market in line with Swissmedic requirements?
  • Who installs the system, connects DICOM to my practice software and trains the team?
  • What is the response time for service in my region, and where is the nearest engineer?
  • What is the trade-in value of my current system?
